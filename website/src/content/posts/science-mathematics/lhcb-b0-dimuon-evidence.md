# LHCb finds first single-experiment evidence for a one-in-ten-billion beauty-meson decay

Status: Preprint · Fully AI generated. Sources may be wrong, please [open an issue on GitHub](https://github.com/AbuCodingAI/tkdb/issues).

## In Plain English

Physicists at CERN's LHCb experiment say they have spotted, for the first time in a single experiment, a beauty particle called a B⁰ meson turning into a muon and an antimuon — a decay thought to happen only about once in every ten billion times [S1][S2]. The signal sits at 3.2 standard deviations, enough for "evidence" in particle physics but not the 5-sigma bar that counts as a discovery [S1][S2][S3]. The rate matches the Standard Model, so this is a precision check rather than a claim of new particles [S1][S3]. The catch: the paper is a preprint, not yet peer-reviewed, and an earlier 2015 claim of the same decay did not hold up when more data arrived [S2][S4].

## Key Findings

- **First single-experiment evidence for B⁰ → μ⁺μ⁻.** In a paper posted as arXiv:2611.00230 (CERN-EP-2026-287 / LHCb-PAPER-2026-053) and presented at a CERN seminar on 3 November 2026, the LHCb collaboration reports an excess consistent with B⁰ → μ⁺μ⁻ at 3.2 standard deviations in a combined Run 1–3 fit, and 3.0σ from the Run 3 sample alone [S1][S2][S5]. Cambridge's release states the results have not been peer-reviewed; the preprint is marked submitted to *Physical Review X* [S1][S4].
- **Combined branching fractions.** The simultaneous fit gives B(Bs⁰ → μ⁺μ⁻) = (3.25^{+0.26}_{-0.25} ^{+0.13}_{-0.12}) × 10⁻⁹ and B(B⁰ → μ⁺μ⁻) = (1.14^{+0.42}_{-0.39} ^{+0.06}_{-0.05}) × 10⁻¹⁰, with the first uncertainty statistical and the second systematic [S1]. Physics World paraphrases the central values as about 3.3 decays per billion Bs⁰ mesons and about 1.1 per ten billion B⁰ mesons [S3].
- **Agrees with the Standard Model.** The paper quotes SM predictions of (3.66 ± 0.14) × 10⁻⁹ and (1.03 ± 0.05) × 10⁻¹⁰ respectively, and states that all reported measurements agree with those expectations [S1]. That agreement is useful for constraining new-physics ideas built to explain other B-decay anomalies, but it is not itself a beyond-Standard-Model signal [S1][S3].
- **Most precise Bs⁰ → μ⁺μ⁻ measurement.** The same analysis updates the already-observed Bs⁰ → μ⁺μ⁻ channel to world-leading precision and reports a combined effective lifetime τμμ = 1.60 ± 0.16 ± 0.01 ps [S1][S2].
- **Why now.** CERN and the paper credit the 2024 LHCb Upgrade I detector, 18 fb⁻¹ of 13.6 TeV Run 3 data from 2024–2025 (about five times the earlier collection rate), machine-learning background suppression, and combination with 9 fb⁻¹ of Run 1–2 data [S1][S2].

**What is established:** a collaboration preprint and seminar claiming ≥3σ evidence from one experiment, with the numbers above [S1][S2]. **What is not:** peer review, a 5σ observation, or independent confirmation by CMS or ATLAS [S2][S3][S4].

## How It Works

1. **A "golden" rare decay.** In the Standard Model, B⁰ (a beauty antiquark bound to a down quark) can annihilate into μ⁺μ⁻ only through suppressed quantum loops, so the predicted rate is about thirty times smaller than the already-rare Bs⁰ → μ⁺μ⁻ channel [S1][S2]. Unknown heavy particles could change that rate, which is why the channel has been hunted since the 1980s [S2][S5].
2. **Pick two muons out of a huge background.** At the LHC the interesting decays are swamped by unrelated muon pairs. LHCb uses its muon system and a multivariate classifier to keep candidates that look like two muons from a single displaced vertex a few millimetres from the collision point [S1][S3][S5].
3. **Count against known calibration decays.** Branching fractions are measured relative to abundant normalisation modes such as B⁰ → K⁺π⁻ and B⁺ → J/ψK⁺, then turned into absolute rates [S1].
4. **Combine Run 3 with older data.** Run 3 alone yields 3.0σ for B⁰ → μ⁺μ⁻; adding the earlier LHCb samples raises the significance to 3.2σ by Wilks' theorem, checked with pseudoexperiments [S1][S2].

## Implications

If the signal survives and climbs to 5σ, it would be the rarest beauty-particle decay yet observed and a clean probe of physics at energy scales far above those the LHC can produce directly [S2][S4]. Because the measured rates sit on the Standard Model prediction, the result already squeezes models that used large new contributions in these dimuon channels to explain lingering anomalies in other b → sℓ⁺ℓ⁻ decays [S1][S3]. Outside theorist Robert Fleischer called the measurement impressive and long-awaited, while stressing that 3.2σ is still well below discovery [S3]. CERN points to the High-Luminosity LHC around 2030 and a further LHCb upgrade as the path to many more of these events [S2].

## Open Questions

- Will the B⁰ → μ⁺μ⁻ excess reach 5σ with more Run 3 or Run 4 data, or fade as the 2015 claim did [S1][S2]?
- Do CMS or ATLAS confirm the same branching fractions with independent detectors and analyses [S2][S3]?
- After peer review at *Physical Review X*, do any central numbers or significance claims change [S1][S4]?
- How tightly do the new rates constrain specific new-physics scenarios built around other B-anomaly measurements [S1][S3]?
